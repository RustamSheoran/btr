# 35-Book Classics Reading Roadmap

A high-ROI reading path for a CS student who wants to develop English comprehension, expression, philosophical reasoning, psychological insight, historical perspective, cultural literacy, and understanding of people.

The order is intentionally designed as a progression: accessible works first, then ancient foundations, increasingly complex psychology and society, political philosophy, Kant, Shakespeare, existentialism, and broader civilization/history.

---

## 1. Animal Farm [X]

**Author:** George Orwell

**About:** A short political allegory about revolution, propaganda, power, corruption, and how political ideals can be manipulated. It is an easy entry point into serious literature and political thought.

---

## 2. The Picture of Dorian Gray

**Author:** Oscar Wilde

**About:** A novel about beauty, pleasure, morality, vanity, identity, and the consequences of pursuing a life without moral restraint. It also provides an excellent introduction to literary prose and symbolism.

---

## 3. The Great Gatsby

**Author:** F. Scott Fitzgerald

**About:** A short novel exploring wealth, class, ambition, social status, love, illusion, and the gap between what people want and reality.

---

## 4. The Metamorphosis

**Author:** Franz Kafka

**About:** A short, strange story about alienation, identity, family obligation, work, and what happens when a person becomes unable to fulfill the role society expects from them.

---

## 5. Meditations

**Author:** Marcus Aurelius

**About:** Personal reflections on Stoicism, self-control, mortality, responsibility, rationality, adversity, and how a person should conduct themselves when circumstances cannot be controlled.

---

## 6. The Odyssey

**Author:** Homer

**About:** An ancient Greek epic about Odysseus's long journey home after the Trojan War. It explores heroism, loyalty, temptation, intelligence, family, hospitality, fate, and the ancient Greek worldview.

---

## 7. The Iliad

**Author:** Homer

**About:** An epic centered on the Trojan War and the conflict between Achilles and Agamemnon. It examines honor, rage, war, mortality, leadership, pride, friendship, and the relationship between humans and gods.

---

## 8. The Three Theban Plays

**Author:** Sophocles

**About:** Three Greek tragedies—Oedipus Rex, Oedipus at Colonus, and Antigone—dealing with fate, moral responsibility, political authority, family loyalty, law, pride, and the limits of human knowledge.

---

## 9. The Republic

**Author:** Plato

**About:** One of the foundational works of Western philosophy. Plato investigates justice, politics, education, knowledge, human nature, leadership, and what an ideal society might look like.

---

## 10. Nicomachean Ethics

**Author:** Aristotle

**About:** Aristotle's investigation of how humans should live. It develops ideas about virtue, happiness, character, friendship, reason, moderation, and the good life.

---

## 11. Crime and Punishment

**Author:** Fyodor Dostoevsky

**About:** A psychological novel about a poor student who commits murder and then struggles with guilt, morality, pride, rationalization, isolation, and redemption.

---

## 12. Notes from Underground

**Author:** Fyodor Dostoevsky

**About:** A short psychological and philosophical work examining self-consciousness, resentment, irrational behavior, free will, ego, alienation, and the human tendency to act against its own interests.

---

## 13. Pride and Prejudice

**Author:** Jane Austen

**About:** A novel about relationships, social class, reputation, marriage, first impressions, pride, prejudice, and the ways people misinterpret one another.

---

## 14. Jane Eyre

**Author:** Charlotte Brontë

**About:** A coming-of-age novel exploring identity, independence, morality, love, social class, gender expectations, and the struggle to preserve personal integrity.

---

## 15. The Death of Ivan Ilyich

**Author:** Leo Tolstoy

**About:** A short novel about a successful man's confrontation with illness and death. It examines status, superficiality, regret, authenticity, mortality, and what actually makes a life meaningful.

---

## 16. The Brothers Karamazov

**Author:** Fyodor Dostoevsky

**About:** A huge philosophical and psychological novel centered on family conflict, morality, faith, doubt, free will, responsibility, suffering, reason, and the problem of evil.

---

## 17. Anna Karenina

**Author:** Leo Tolstoy

**About:** A complex novel about love, marriage, social expectations, jealousy, family, morality, status, work, and the different ways people search for meaning and happiness.

---

## 18. The Prince

**Author:** Niccolò Machiavelli

**About:** A compact analysis of political power, leadership, statecraft, manipulation, reputation, military strength, and the practical realities of acquiring and maintaining political authority.

---

## 19. 1984

**Author:** George Orwell

**About:** A dystopian novel about totalitarianism, surveillance, propaganda, censorship, political control, language, historical revision, and the relationship between truth and power.

---

## 20. The Count of Monte Cristo

**Author:** Alexandre Dumas

**About:** A massive adventure and revenge novel about betrayal, imprisonment, transformation, wealth, manipulation, justice, revenge, and the psychological consequences of pursuing vengeance.

---

## 21. Les Misérables

**Author:** Victor Hugo

**About:** A sweeping novel about poverty, law, justice, morality, redemption, social inequality, political revolution, compassion, and the relationship between individual lives and society.

---

## 22. War and Peace

**Author:** Leo Tolstoy

**About:** A vast novel combining family life with the Napoleonic Wars. It explores history, war, leadership, social class, love, death, individual agency, and how ordinary people experience enormous historical events.

---

## 23. Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals

**Author:** Immanuel Kant

**About:** Kant develops his theory of morality, focusing on duty, rationality, free will, moral intention, universal principles, and the categorical imperative. It is one of the best entry points into Kant's philosophy.

---

## 24. Hamlet

**Author:** William Shakespeare

**About:** A tragedy about revenge, uncertainty, deception, grief, morality, identity, political corruption, and the difficulty of acting when one cannot be certain what is true.

---

## 25. Macbeth

**Author:** William Shakespeare

**About:** A tragedy about ambition, power, prophecy, guilt, violence, manipulation, and psychological collapse.

---

## 26. The Divine Comedy

**Author:** Dante Alighieri

**About:** Dante's journey through Hell, Purgatory, and Paradise. It presents the medieval Christian worldview while exploring morality, sin, justice, love, politics, philosophy, symbolism, and the nature of human existence.

---

## 27. Man's Search for Meaning

**Author:** Viktor E. Frankl

**About:** Frankl combines his experiences in Nazi concentration camps with his psychological ideas about suffering, meaning, resilience, responsibility, and the human capacity to choose one's response to circumstances.

---

## 28. The Stranger

**Author:** Albert Camus

**About:** A short existential novel about alienation, absurdity, morality, death, social expectations, and the search—or refusal to search—for meaning.

---

## 29. The Trial

**Author:** Franz Kafka

**About:** A surreal novel about a man trapped in an opaque legal system. It explores bureaucracy, institutional power, helplessness, guilt, uncertainty, and the absurdity of systems that individuals cannot understand or control.

---

## 30. On Liberty

**Author:** John Stuart Mill

**About:** A foundational argument for individual liberty. Mill examines when society or the state should be allowed to interfere with an individual's choices and why freedom of thought and expression matter.

---

## 31. The Social Contract

**Author:** Jean-Jacques Rousseau

**About:** Rousseau investigates political legitimacy, freedom, authority, government, sovereignty, and the relationship between individuals and the collective political community.

---

## 32. On the Origin of Species

**Author:** Charles Darwin

**About:** Darwin's foundational explanation of evolution through natural selection. It develops a powerful example of how complex biological diversity can arise through cumulative natural processes.

---

## 33. The Autobiography of Benjamin Franklin

**Author:** Benjamin Franklin

**About:** Franklin's account of his life, education, self-improvement, work, reputation, discipline, relationships, and intellectual development. It provides a practical perspective on self-education and personal development.

---

## 34. The Muqaddimah

**Author:** Ibn Khaldun

**About:** A pioneering work of social and historical analysis examining civilization, group cohesion, political power, economics, taxation, cities, culture, and why societies rise and decline.

---

## 35. The Analects

**Author:** Confucius

**About:** A collection of sayings and discussions associated with Confucius, focusing on ethics, education, leadership, social relationships, personal conduct, responsibility, and the cultivation of character.

---

# Optional Advanced Extensions

These are not required for the core 35, but are worth reading if the philosophical sections particularly interest you.

## A. Critique of Pure Reason

**Author:** Immanuel Kant

**About:** Kant investigates the limits and structure of human knowledge, asking what reason can know independently of experience and how concepts such as space, time, causality, and experience are possible.

**Difficulty:** Very high.

---

## B. Critique of Practical Reason

**Author:** Immanuel Kant

**About:** A deeper development of Kant's moral philosophy, focusing on practical reason, freedom, morality, duty, and the foundations of ethical action.

**Difficulty:** High.

---

## C. Beyond Good and Evil

**Author:** Friedrich Nietzsche

**About:** Nietzsche challenges conventional assumptions about morality, truth, religion, philosophers, power, and human motivation. It is a major transition toward modern critiques of traditional morality.

**Difficulty:** High.

---

# Suggested Reading Pace

At **2–3 hours per day**, the goal should be consistent reading rather than speed.

A reasonable overall target is approximately **1–2 years** for the complete 35-book path if the books are read carefully, difficult passages are reread, and unfamiliar vocabulary or historical references are occasionally investigated.

The shortest books can take only a few days. The largest works—especially *The Brothers Karamazov*, *Anna Karenina*, *The Count of Monte Cristo*, *Les Misérables*, and *War and Peace*—can take several weeks.

For philosophy, measure progress by understanding the argument rather than pages completed.

For novels, focus on understanding characters, motives, themes, historical context, and the author's assumptions rather than trying to extract a lesson from every paragraph.

# The 10 Highest-ROI Books

If the complete 35-book path becomes too demanding, prioritize:

1. **Meditations**
2. **The Odyssey**
3. **The Republic**
4. **Crime and Punishment**
5. **The Brothers Karamazov**
6. **Anna Karenina**
7. **1984**
8. **Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals**
9. **The Death of Ivan Ilyich**
10. **The Muqaddimah**

These give an especially strong combination of philosophy, psychology, literature, politics, history, civilization, and understanding of human behavior.
